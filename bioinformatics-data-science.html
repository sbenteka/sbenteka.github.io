Computational Research

Computation as part of biological reasoning

I use bioinformatics and data science as tools for asking biological questions rather than as an endpoint in themselves. My work spans environmental microbiology, microbial ecology, comparative stress biology, genomics, and transcriptomics, with an emphasis on connecting quantitative patterns to ecological or mechanistic interpretation.

Sequence-based biology

End-to-end analysis of genomic, transcriptomic, amplicon, and metagenomic data. My work includes read-quality assessment and trimming, taxonomic assignment, genome and transcriptome mapping, targeted transcript reconstruction in non-model organisms, abundance estimation, and comparative sequence analysis.

Quantitative analysis

Statistical analysis of high-dimensional biological datasets, including differential expression, interaction modeling, abundance comparisons, PCA, clustering, functional enrichment, and publication-ready visualization. I routinely connect quantitative patterns back to experimental design and biological mechanism.

R DESeq2 ggplot2 Jalview Adobe Illustrator

Reproducible workflows

Building reproducible pipelines for large sequencing datasets, from raw-read QC and preprocessing through alignment, quantification, statistical analysis, and figure generation. I routinely execute and troubleshoot these workflows in HPC environments and adapt them for non-model organisms and custom genome annotations.

Examples Across Research Systems

Methods adapted to the biological question

My computational work has developed across different research systems rather than around a single data type, from microbial population ecology to high-dimensional sequencing datasets.

Microbial ecology & targeted sequencing

In my work on Frankia-actinorhizal symbioses, I combined quantitative PCR, targeted sequencing, comparative sequence analysis, and phylogenetic approaches to characterize microbial populations in soils and root nodules.

This involved comparing the abundance and diversity of environmental populations with those recovered from host-associated niches, integrating multiple molecular datasets to answer ecological questions about distribution and host selection.

The emphasis was not simply detecting sequences, but relating quantitative and phylogenetic patterns to microbial ecology and symbiotic selection.

High-dimensional omics

My more recent work has expanded into genome-scale and transcriptome-scale analysis, including RNA-seq quality control and preprocessing, splice-aware alignment to a non-model tardigrade genome, read quantification, targeted transcript reconstruction and annotation refinement, differential expression, multivariate analysis, clustering, and comparative stress-response analysis.

These projects require integration of large sequencing datasets with experimental design, high-performance computing, statistical modeling, custom annotation decisions, and biological interpretation across complex treatment structures.

Across systems, the goal remains the same: use computation to reveal biological structure that is difficult to see from raw measurements alone.
