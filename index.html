About

I am a microbial ecologist and microbiologist interested in how microorganisms and other biological systems survive, interact, and adapt under challenging environmental conditions. My research spans microbial ecology, environmental genomics, extremophile biology, symbiosis, and space biology, with a particular interest in the biological mechanisms that enable life to persist at environmental limits.

A major focus of my work has been microorganisms from arid, nutrient-limited, rocky, saline, and otherwise stressful habitats. I combine cultivation, physiological characterization, genomics, and comparative analysis to investigate microbial diversity, adaptation, and the genetic traits associated with environmental resilience. My work on plant-associated systems has also examined how microbial abundance, diversity, and host selection shape nitrogen-fixing Frankia-actinorhizal symbioses.

Computational biology is an integral part of my research. I routinely work with genomic, transcriptomic, metagenomic, and other high-dimensional biological datasets, using reproducible workflows in R, Bash, high-performance computing environments, and established bioinformatics tools. I am particularly interested in connecting quantitative patterns with biological mechanisms rather than treating computation as an endpoint in itself.

Across these areas, my broader research interest is understanding biology at the limits of life, from extreme terrestrial environments to space-associated conditions, and using integrated experimental and computational approaches to reveal how organisms respond to environmental stress.
